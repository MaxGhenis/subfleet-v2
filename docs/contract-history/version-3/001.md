C-6.9, C-6.11, C-6.12 (2026-10-12, stateful admission properties, findings 5 and 6, `docs/reports/2026-10-12-admission-properties.md`): a job held behind an older one while its capacity clock runs still holds back the later jobs of its tier that compete with it, with the demand it has while that clock runs (a pinned retry's pair, or its own once a look has let the pin go). Before, it registered as no waiter, so in a pool with a count cap a younger job that competed with it and not with the older job was placed past it. A lease wait still holds nobody, nor does a job no lane can admit, whose `behind-older-job` hold now carries `for_good` (a lease waiter's too). Admission asks C-11.8 of such a job, and of any job on its clock, with the lanes its attempts excluded merged in as its route merges them (C-4.5): a lane re-enrolled since a `limited` attempt is excluded under its successor's id, which the stored exclusions do not name (review of #201 r1). A job whose exclusions cannot be merged (a stored one that is not a name) holds nobody, as the `route` wait its route's preparation settles it into holds nobody; a retry whose pin a look let go is settled there rather than held behind an older job (review of #201 r2). The added holds last only while that job's clock runs: held behind, it gets no look and so no new clock. A transient retry whose pin was let go is the exception C-6.12 already makes: its due look can let the pin go again and set a new clock, so it holds its younger competitors for as long as the older job waits and its pair stays refused.

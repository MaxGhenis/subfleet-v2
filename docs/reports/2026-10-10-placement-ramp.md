## R6: why the differential failed, and the fix

On `b6fdd0b28` the explicit example (`queue=[("ordinary","trivial",None)]*3, enabled=False, cap=None, initial=36, advance=0, gate="disk", restart=True`) failed with `passes[1].decisions[0].decision_json (JSON).policy_hash: current '0f80b29d…' != previous '3f16d40b…'`. The cause was the fixture, not admission.

- **Mechanism.** Both runs restarted their daemon after `(service.root / "policy.json").write_text(json.dumps(service.policy))`. `load_policy` adds `_policy_hash` and `_policy_path` to the map it returns (`subfleet/policy.py:718`; release/217 does the same at its `policy.py:678`), and `_policy_path` is the state root's own path. Each run has its own temporary root (`ramp-differential0`, `ramp-differential1`), so the two rewritten files differed by that path. The daemon's decision hash is the SHA-256 of the file's bytes (`subfleet/daemon.py:752`; C-11.1), so the hashes differed. `snapshot` replaces the state root in its JSON text, but a hash of the path cannot be normalized that way.
- **Reproduced outside pytest.** Loading the bundled policy in two roots gives `47e1c511…` in both; rewriting each loaded map with `json.dumps` gives `f5791666…` and `ba755e82…`, and its only run-specific keys are `_policy_hash` and `_policy_path`.
- **Not a change in behaviour.** Production never serializes a loaded policy. The daemon writes `policy.json` only by copying the bundled bytes when the file is absent (`subfleet/daemon.py:750`), and nothing reloads the policy after start. Running release/217's own `policy.py` (from `4c6a8a9c`) beside the current loader, the two agree in all nine hash readings (both loaders, both roots, `policy_hash`, raw SHA-256) for each of five ramp-absent files: the bundled default (`47e1c511…`), the bundle without `admission` (`bb9a57a6…`), an operator-tuned bundle (`63c4e25f…`), and the R6 restart files for an absent ramp with disk (`35148df0…`) and machine (`f5c09433…`) gates. On the pre-fix restart files, release/217's loader also gives the two roots different hashes (`e3c07196…` and `6833b82f…`), exactly as the current loader does.
- **Fix.** `save_policy` writes the policy as an operator's file would hold it, without the loader's `_`-prefixed metadata, so both runs load identical bytes and the differential compares policy hashes raw rather than canonicalizing them. After every pass the test also asserts that `policy_digest`, the loaded `_policy_hash`, and every new decision's `policy_hash` equal the SHA-256 of the active `policy.json`.
- **A second fixture clock, found by the first pass after that fix.** With Hypothesis capped at 3 examples, the explicit example passed, but a generated one (`queue=[("ordinary","trivial",None)]*2, cap=1, initial=0, advance=0`, either gate, either restart) failed once: `passes[0].decisions[1].evaluated_at: current '2026-10-11T03:24:25Z' != previous '2026-10-11T03:24:26Z'`, and Hypothesis reported it flaky. Those are wall-clock times; the fake clock read `2026-10-09T16:33:00Z`. With `cap=1` the second job is held `fleet-full`, and that hold writes its decision through `Store.add_decision`, which stamps `subfleet.store.utc_now()` (`subfleet/store.py:786`). Its next look comes from `scheduler.waiting_metadata` called without `now` (`subfleet/daemon.py:5446`), which reads `datetime.now` in `scheduler`. Neither clock was patched. The current and frozen passes reach that code identically (the frozen copy calls `add_decision` at `tests/fixtures/admission_ramp_before.py:588`), so the runs differed only when they straddled a wall-clock second. `fake_clock` now patches `datetime` in `scheduler`, `store` and `capacity` as well, and the differential asserts that every new decision is stamped with the fake clock, so such a leak now fails every time.
- **Separate guarantee.** `tests/unit/test_policy_ramp.py:29` asserts that a policy file without `admission.ramp` (the bundle, the bundle without `admission`, and an operator-tuned bundle) hashes to the SHA-256 of its exact bytes through both `load_policy` and `policy_hash`, at two different paths, with loading writing nothing back: release/217's definition. `tests/unit/test_policy_ramp.py:17` keeps the bundled default at the pre-ramp hash `47e1c5111b9cef28751257667cd8aa2eb9ba0ac39ef7e55beff30879a429e49c`, identical to release/217's bundle.

## Fractional allowances

The rule commits a placement while `used < per_minute × window_s / 60`. With a fractional allowance that comparison admits the allowance's ceiling: 12 per minute over a 1-second window (allowance 0.2) admits one start a second, 60 a minute, five times the stated rate; 1 per minute over 1 second also admits 60 a minute. Rather than document a ramp that can run 60 times faster than its own `per_minute`, the loader now refuses every policy whose `per_minute × window_s` is not a multiple of 60, enabled or not, naming `admission.ramp.window_s`. No released policy can be affected: release/217's loader rejects `admission.ramp` as an unknown admission key, and the shipped 12/60 setting is whole. The pacing code itself still applies the literal comparison to raw settings that never pass the loader (only tests build those), so it is unchanged.

For every accepted policy R1 is exact: no window of `window_s` seconds holds more than `K` non-priority starts. R2's bounds for whole `K`: from an empty window, n jobs place within `(ceil(n / K) − 1) × window_s` seconds plus one pass, less than `ceil(n / per_minute)` minutes. From a window already holding up to `K` starts, they place within `ceil(n / K) × window_s` plus one pass. That is exactly `ceil(n / per_minute)` minutes for the 60-second window, and at most `window_s` more for any window: at 3 per minute over 40 seconds, three jobs behind a full window can take 80 seconds. A priority burst above `K` and continuing priority arrivals delay ordinary work further, and changing eligibility over time can change the baseline order; C-6.18 states these qualifications.

## Contract clause text

- **C-6.18** (placement ramp, 2026-10-10, D-MG1) `admission.ramp` is off by default (`enabled: false`, `per_minute: 12`, `window_s: 60`); the hub opts in through its live policy. The loader accepts a boolean enabled flag and finite positive integers for rate and window, rejects booleans and floats as integers, and rejects unknown keys naming the exact setting. It also requires `per_minute * window_s` to be a multiple of 60, enabled or not, and otherwise refuses the policy naming `admission.ramp.window_s`, so each window's allowance `K = per_minute * window_s / 60` is a whole number of starts. The rule below admits a fractional allowance's ceiling: 12 per minute over a 1-second window would place one job each second, 60 a minute. A non-priority detached placement of any accepted non-turn job kind (`dispatch`, `resume`, `revive`, `gate-review`, or `handoff`), including a retry, commits only while the number of detached non-probe attempts whose `reserved_at` is inside the last `window_s` seconds is below `K`, so while the clock does not run backwards no window of `window_s` seconds holds more than `K` non-priority starts. Each enabled detached pass reads attempt rows once and adds its own committed placements immediately, so a daemon restart cannot grant a fresh burst; completed, failed and recovered reservations still count until their timestamps leave the window. The window is open at its old edge: a reservation exactly `window_s` seconds old no longer counts. The ramp keeps admission's priority and FIFO order and stops ordinary placements once the window is full. C-6.16 priority jobs bypass the hold as they do C-6.13's guard, but their committed placements count in the same window and slow ordinary work that follows. Attended turns never read, wait on or count against the ramp; probes are exempt and never enter its ledger. Held jobs have a `ramp` reason in the pass's in-memory holds, with no lane lease and no capacity backoff, and are re-evaluated next pass. `why <job>` reports "placement ramp: N of M starts used in the last W s; next slot at <time>"; status carries one ramp line. The existing periodic control loop (`_control`, `tick_s`, `_schedule("admission", ...)`) runs admission when the next slot opens without an unrelated event. Retention pins jobs with recent detached attempts until those starts leave the window, rechecking those pins inside its deleting transaction. Disabled or absent policy reads no ramp history, counts no placement and preserves existing admission decisions exactly; a policy file without `admission.ramp` keeps the policy hash of its exact bytes (C-11.1), and the shipped `default_policy.json` keeps its pre-ramp bytes. With free capacity and no continuing priority arrivals, every unchanged eligible finite queue eventually places in admission order. From an empty window, n jobs place within `(ceil(n / K) - 1) * window_s` seconds plus one pass, which is less than `ceil(n / per_minute)` minutes. From a window already holding up to `K` starts, they place within `ceil(n / K) * window_s` seconds plus one pass: exactly `ceil(n / per_minute)` minutes for the default 60-second window, and at most `window_s` seconds more than that for any window (at 3 per minute over 40 seconds, 3 jobs behind a full window can take 80 seconds). A priority burst that leaves more than `K` starts in the window delays ordinary work until enough of them expire.

# Detached placement ramp (D-MG1)

Implemented the hub's opt-in `admission.ramp` design. Defaults are `enabled: false`, `per_minute: 12`, `window_s: 60`. The machine-guard thresholds and disk floor defaults are unchanged. Work and commits are confined to the assigned workspace; branch `feat/admission-ramp` lives in `.git-local` because shared Git metadata is read-only. No push or PR was created.

- New policy, gate, fake-daemon, retention and frozen-baseline differential tests; every test-module basename is unique.

Scope follows C-6.9 and the explicit exemptions: every accepted non-turn job is detached, and a gate round is class `session`. Restricting the ramp to the stored `dispatch` kind would let gate reviews and continuation jobs bypass holds and consume no allowance. The implementation therefore covers `dispatch`, `resume`, `revive`, `gate-review`, and `handoff`; priority affects holding, and attended turns and probes remain outside the ledger.

Liveness uses the existing periodic `_control` loop at `subfleet/daemon.py:3673`, `tick_s` (50 ms by default), and `_schedule("admission", self._admit, paced=True)`. This is the same mechanism that rechecks disk TTL expiry and capacity backoff; no unrelated event is needed. The fake control-loop test runs at 59 and 60 seconds with notifications disabled and asserts placement at expiry.

## Contract clause text

- **C-6.18** (placement ramp, 2026-10-10, D-MG1) `admission.ramp` is off by default (`enabled: false`, `per_minute: 12`, `window_s: 60`); the hub opts in through its live policy. The loader accepts a boolean enabled flag and finite positive integers for rate and window, rejects booleans and floats as integers, and rejects unknown keys naming the exact setting. A non-priority detached placement of any accepted non-turn job kind (`dispatch`, `resume`, `revive`, `gate-review`, or `handoff`), including a retry, commits only while the number of detached non-probe attempts whose `reserved_at` is inside the last `window_s` seconds is below `per_minute * window_s / 60`. Each enabled detached pass reads attempt rows once and adds its own committed placements immediately, so a daemon restart cannot grant a fresh burst; completed, failed and recovered reservations still count until their timestamps leave the window. The window is open at its old edge: a reservation exactly `window_s` seconds old no longer counts. The ramp keeps admission's priority and FIFO order and stops ordinary placements once the window is full. C-6.16 priority jobs bypass the hold as they do C-6.13's guard, but their committed placements count in the same window and slow ordinary work that follows. Attended turns never read, wait on or count against the ramp; probes are exempt and never enter its ledger. Held jobs have a `ramp` reason in the pass's in-memory holds, with no lane lease and no capacity backoff, and are re-evaluated next pass. `why <job>` reports "placement ramp: N of M starts used in the last W s; next slot at <time>"; status carries one ramp line. The existing periodic control loop (`_control`, `tick_s`, `_schedule("admission", ...)`) runs admission when the next slot opens without an unrelated event. Retention pins jobs with recent detached attempts until those starts leave the window, rechecking those pins inside its deleting transaction. Disabled or absent policy reads no ramp history, counts no placement and preserves existing admission decisions exactly. With free capacity and no continuing priority arrivals, every unchanged eligible finite queue eventually places in admission order. From an empty window, n jobs place within `ceil(n / per_minute)` minutes plus one pass; the default 60-second window also meets that bound with a prefilled window. Longer prefilled windows can add an initial wait of up to `window_s`. The specified pre-placement comparison admits the ceiling of a fractional allowance; the exact R1 numeric bound therefore holds for integral allowances, including the shipped 12-per-60-second setting. For example, 12 per minute with a 1-second window has allowance 0.2 and permits one start, so the literal rule and an unrounded R1 cannot both hold for every valid policy.

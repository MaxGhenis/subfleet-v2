A retention hold names its fence keys as `retiring` and the folder checked, and `why` says retention is removing the finished job's tree (C-6.11). A turn's canonical actual run folder is fenced too, with a reader row when it differs from its Git hold. Quarantine cleanup keeps live turn cwds and turn rows, including quarantined attempts, and fences its location before reclaiming it. An in-place writer's folder is its write target, its checkout's top level spelled once at submit (C-6.5), and a fence on that folder itself is its own `worktree:` key, contested as before; an allocated writer's write target is the worktree admission allocates for it (C-6.6). A read-only detached job's folder, and an allocated writer's source folder, is its workdir spelled once at submission (`folder` in `job.submitted`). Every detached admission look also spells where the workdir resolves now (`_detached_folders`, outside transactions), so a workdir replaced by a symlink into another fenced tree is checked there as well as on its recorded folder; no spelling is cached between looks. A legacy detached job with no recorded folder or write target uses that current spelling with NFC normalization and casefolding (`folders.retiring_folded`), including when quarantine has made its original names unspellable. This folded comparison can conservatively hold a legacy job on a case-equivalent name on a volume that distinguishes case. Such a job waits through the retirement. An admission look that reaches the early fence check reads the turn's recorded Git/read folder or the detached job's recorded and current folders before `_workspace`; when it sees a fence, it rechecks it in the transaction that records the wait and runs no workspace Git. Operator holds, standing pin refusals, disk and machine checks and pool ordering can hold a job before this check. A fence acquired after the early read is still checked in the reserving transaction, though workspace preparation may already have run; a turn's actual run cwd is calculated after workspace preparation and fenced there too. A fence found after the pass's lease census is counted as seen, so its release brings the next look forward (C-6.10). An early fenced priority job keeps its priority place across tiers when a pool cap applies (C-6.16). A queued job whose recorded workdir matches the allocated worktree or a folder inside it keeps that tree at the commit (`worktree-in-use`, while the job has not ended), which rolls the retirement back; submission records a job's workdir in its one spelling (`folders.canonical`), so a workdir typed in another case or Unicode form than its volume stores, or through the Data volume's firmlink (`/System/Volumes/Data/…`), still names the tree it is in, as long as every name in it can be looked up then; a name that cannot (gone, or under a directory that cannot be searched at that moment) stays as typed. Of the leases on a folder above a turn's or a detached job's, only retention's fence holds it, never a detached writer's (C-6.5). Read-only detached jobs and allocated writers take no lease on their source folder and wait there only for retention; writable turns and in-place writers still obey C-6.5's contention on their exact write target. A `worktree:` lease held by anything but the job's own retirement (`retention:<job>`) keeps the job as `worktree-lease`: one on its recorded worktree keeps any job, and one on a folder inside it keeps a job with its own allocated worktree, as a turn row does (a detached in-place writer in a repository nested there holds `worktree:<that repository>`, and its quarantined attempt leaves its job `lost` with the lease kept); the retirement transaction reads those leases on the canonical worktree and every folder inside it (`folders.exclusive_inside`, one key range) before taking the fence, and the commit transaction reads them again on the journal's canonical spelling. Known limits: names submission cannot look up (the tree already in quarantine, or a directory above it briefly unsearchable) stay as typed (`folders.spelling`). If a turn's fenced folder, a turn row or a writer lease retains the tree's own name (its job id) in another case than the volume stores, exact admission fence lookups and retirement's row or lease checks can miss that spelling (legacy detached admission without recorded folder data instead folds names as above); once the job has ended, its recorded workdir no longer pins the tree (review of 8a112986; the writer case was executed in the review of c3cf6a55). Folder comparisons use recorded or precomputed strings, with legacy detached admission folding names as above and canonical spelling established outside transactions (`folders.canonical`), never filesystem inspection under the store lock. Pruning archives first (d635, `docs/desktop/retention-archive.md`): a job's files (worktree and job directory, byte for byte as clones or copies, except a tracked file whose raw bytes are a blob a network remote's refs reach in a repository that is not scratch, read back whole, and except regenerable output: inside the entries a tool writes at the top of a worktree directory whose structure says it is a virtualenv, `node_modules`, `__pycache__` (all of it) or a tool cache carrying `CACHEDIR.TAG`, which git tracks nothing in and ignores entirely (names compared NFC-normalized and casefolded) and no nested repository holds, none of them holding a `.git`, the files a rule proves regenerable (an installed distribution's file whose sha256 its RECORD names, from a distribution uv installed from an index or a network URL; bytecode with a magic number beside its source; a tool cache's own files; nothing of `node_modules`), which are deleted with the tree and not restored, while everything else in that directory is archived), its worktree's admin directory and every object that directory names, its salvage commits and baseline (parents of one anchor commit, the only head of a bundle of every commit no network remote holds; a salvage ref whose commit the verified anchor reaches is referenced elsewhere), and its rows (`rows.json`) are written to `$SUBFLEET_HOME/archive/<job>/` and read back before the rows are deleted; after that, an entry is deleted only if its signature (ctime included) is unchanged right before the unlink, or, for a file that had other links when archived and whose ctime alone moved, only if its bytes still hash to the archived sha256, and anything new or changed goes to `$SUBFLEET_HOME/retention-conflicts/<job>/`. A salvage snapshot's skipped paths are protected unless the archive itself holds their bytes and reads them back; a caller's `salvage_referenced_elsewhere` vouch releases only the ref pin, never the file-preservation check. A regular file with neither stored bytes nor a verified omission or regenerable proof keeps the job as `protected`, with `unarchived path` and its path in the result's `deferred` reasons, never `pruned`; this check also runs before committing a resumed archive, whose journal bypasses the builder. A job whose tree another tool holds aside, or whose tree left or came back while it was being retired, is kept. A pass retires the oldest eligible jobs in a bounded batch, without sizing every job first, parks a job whose archive needs more than its slice with its progress (a slice always makes new progress, and parked jobs take turns), defers a busy or failing one (keeping what it archived; a repeated error waits longer), counts a job it cannot measure as decided, and never runs a repository-wide `git worktree prune`; the daemon retains measured sizes and archive progress between passes. While work remains, a completed batch that advances (including a newly cached size, a parked archive slice, publication, partial verified deletion, or a prune) is followed 5 s later; an advancing batch that reaches its deadline warns and raises `TimeoutError`, on C-5.10’s worker clock. A deadline with neither pruning nor advancement marks `TimeoutError`, warns once with the store’s job count, rearms the hour and does not raise. A cancelled pass marks `CancelledError` and rearms the hour even after progress. A completed pass with nothing more, or a batch with no advancement, marks and rearms the hour from its end. In every rearming outcome `_last_maintenance` is set last, after status bookkeeping, so a bookkeeping failure leaves retention due. A deadline limits new starts, never commits of verified archives; even slow pre-selection work permits one job to start and receive its archive slice. A failed batch holder scan marks `ScanFailed` and rearms the hour, even after quarantine, rollback or archive work, to avoid cycling the store against an unavailable scanner. Delivered service notices older than 14 days are pruned first, once per retention call (one bounded batch); a failure there is logged and never stops job retention. Recovery finishes journaled retirements before new selection and prioritizes a job left with a journal-less `retention:<job>` lease. Its events, each pool in its result and the daemon's log lines report the bytes freed (tracked files a remote holds and regenerable output, deleted without a copy) apart from the bytes moved into the archive and the bytes the archive adds (its bundle, manifest, summary and rows, and byte copies), with the net. A job whose baseline no network remote holds, and whose bundle would carry more than `retention.remote_less_history_bytes` (default 64 MiB) of history, is kept; a job registered in a repository inside another job's tree pins that job while it has rows. Retention's children run under the `utility` QoS clamp (C-5.1). Archives are kept until a person removes them.

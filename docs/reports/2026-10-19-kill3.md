## Cause, as executed

The reviewer ran an unrooted shell provider which left a setsid'd lockf waiting
on a scratch lock and exited by itself. Finalization marked the attempt `failed`
without quarantine while lockf was alive. On the review host's macOS 26, the argument
buffer of a hardened-runtime Apple platform binary can hide its environment.
After the provider dies, lockf has no visible marker, group membership or parent
link to the attempt. D-KILL2 remembers the kill protocol's first census, but
self-exit and a provider ending on a turn's SIGINT can reach finalization without
that census. Steady-state inspection previously remembered group members only.

Here, the unchanged release code reproduced both finalization decisions through
the real daemon methods with deterministic kernel observations: both attempts
became `failed` while the hidden runner remained. The inspection unit regression
also failed because `counted_identities` was absent. Two native cwd retention
regressions removed their owned worktree while our scratch observer was alive,
including the `/tmp` spelling; the owned-attempt census incorrectly verified
empty. The in-place cases passed before the fix. These are six intended baseline
failures across two separate lock holds; the between-inspection limitation passed.

The three real-daemon shell/lockf cases skipped: this sandbox denies `/bin/ps`
and both subprocess and in-process boot-identity sysctl reads. Native libproc cwd
reads of our own scratch children are permitted and were exercised. No live job
was targeted. The stop regression drives the real `TurnRunner` timers with a
controlled clock: SIGINT at 10 s, before close at 20 s or kill at 30 s. Its
deterministic variant is a `kind='turn'` job and rejects premature relay release;
the real shell variant uses the dispatch harness. A full conversation-service
stop through the relay was not run.

## Fix and contract

`Daemon._record_owned`, called by `_inspect_running` for both the shared-table
and fresh-table fallback paths, now also walks descendants outside the group.
An in-memory parent index costs one table pass, including deep trees; absent and
zombie parents remain walk anchors. Matching recorded survivors seed the walk,
and different births do not. New readable identities are persisted under
`counted_identities` with `attempt.counted_recorded`, merging with the stored
evidence. The next deciding census uses D-KILL2's existing persisted seeds.
Repeated observations write nothing. Only group members become owned; no
signal authority is added and no marker scan, responsibility scan or extra
process listing is added to inspection.

A deciding attempt census and retention now hold a job-owned worktree while a
live process has its cwd inside it. The native `PROC_PIDVNODEPATHINFO` read starts
no process; both paths are resolved before component-boundary comparison, so
`/tmp` and `/private/tmp` agree. Evidence says only `pid <pid>: cwd in worktree`.
Observers, including an operator's shell or editor, never become identity seeds
or signal targets. In-place workdirs and probes are excluded. Retention checks
before moving the tree and at holder checks on a moved tree, including resumed
retirements; a native cwd hold needs no lsof. An unreadable required scan holds
rather than verifies empty. An unreaped zombie must not create a global hold.

C-5.5, C-5.12 and C-13.4 and their change log are amended in the implementation
commit. C-5.5's D-KILL2 sentence now follows “Snapshot shapes from a different
birth are omitted.” “Its live state” again refers to the responsibility member.
Outside the separate owned-worktree cwd hold, a hidden-marker runner born and
reparented between inspections, before any
inspection or census captures it, remains a `documented_limitation`; both a
deterministic daemon test and a real-daemon test pin it. Cwd reads are snapshots,
so a process entering afterwards remains an observation window.

### Four findings: cause and fix

1. **P2, publication fencing.** The terminal worker treated every worktree
   lease as a cwd hold, including leases an accepted job still needed while
   pushing. It now offers only non-in-place jobs whose export and push have
   settled and whose only leases, across the job and its attempts, are worktree
   keys. The same SQL predicate is rechecked atomically after the observer scan
   before deletion. This explicit readiness rule also rediscovers failed-scan
   holds after restart, which may have no recorded cwd_holders; keying solely
   on evidence would miss them. Other lease paths retain their behavior.
   Tests cover job/attempt holders, other lease kinds, unsettled export/push,
   in-place exclusion and a lease acquired during the scan. The real file://
   push test waits for `_publish_bundle` to enter before ticking the cwd worker,
   then verifies the fence survives and publication settles, for both allocated
   and in-place writers. The existing push/export matrix also gets that tick.
2. **P3, census cost during the wait.** Finalization and start grace ran a full
   census each control tick during the 30 s cwd-only wait. They now gate these
   reads at inspect_interval_s, measured from before the census, as the terminal
   worker does. Receipt/cancel handling stays above that gate. A result other
   than cwd-only clears its pacing; the existing attributed-survivor behavior
   is retained. Ended attempts forget their wait clocks. Tests include both an
   instantaneous read and the reviewer's 254 ms read cost. Native-cwd departure
   tests now advance to a due census, including departure within the window.
3. **P3, containment decisions.** Quarantine resolution and the wall's dead-
   guardian branch still used verified_empty, mixing cwd observers with
   containment. Both now use containment_empty. With cwd_pids={4500},
   --confirm-dead accepts and --force-release salvages as for an empty census;
   the worktree lease, cwd_holders evidence and pid-only notice remain while
   the observer lives. The wall takes `_lost` and completes its bounded wait
   without recovery killed_by or a kill event. Tests forbid both signal APIs,
   check salvage in both modes, and release only after observer departure.
4. **P3, contract drift.** C-5.9 now says the 30 s wait starts over after daemon
   restart and describes its cadence and terminal-worker eligibility. C-5.5
   names the terminal recheck after a verdict. The P2-2 change-log entry says
   counted_absent persists across restart, matching code and its restart entry.
   The restart test preserves the store and discards daemon clocks, proving a
   fresh 30 s wait. The amendment and change log were committed with the code;
   other merged clauses and entries were preserved.

### Six invariants, all covered by tests

- **Verdicts:** cwd-only evidence holds only the worktree and never changes a
  verdict. Native finalization/start-grace/departure, quarantine resolution and
  completed dead-guardian loss controls cover this.
- **No new signal authority:** counted identities and cwd observers never
  authorize or block a signal. The existing signal audit and new quarantine/
  wall tests forbid observer-driven signals; attributed survivors quarantine.
- **Bounded memory:** counted entries stay bounded by the live walk plus the
  latest two distinct inspection tables. Expiry, restart, interrupted-write
  and generated inspection tests exercise this unchanged round-1 rule.
- **Idempotence:** repeating the same inspection table writes nothing. The

- **Birth identity:** a proven recycled pid is not adopted. Counted, recycled-

While work remains, a completed batch that advances (including a newly cached size, a parked archive slice, publication, partial verified deletion, or a prune) is followed 5 s later; an advancing batch that reaches its deadline warns and raises `TimeoutError`, on C-5.10’s worker clock. A deadline with neither pruning nor advancement marks `TimeoutError`, warns once with the store’s job count, rearms the hour and does not raise. A cancelled pass marks `CancelledError` and rearms the hour even after progress. A completed pass with nothing more, or a batch with no advancement, marks and rearms the hour from its end. In every rearming outcome `_last_maintenance` is set last, after status bookkeeping, so a bookkeeping failure leaves retention due. A deadline limits new starts, never commits of verified archives; even slow pre-selection work permits one job to start and receive its archive slice. A failed batch holder scan marks `ScanFailed` and rearms the hour, even after quarantine, rollback or archive work, to avoid cycling the store against an unavailable scanner. Delivered service notices older than 14 days are pruned first, once per retention call (one bounded batch); a failure there is logged and never stops job retention. Recovery finishes journaled retirements before new selection and prioritizes a job left with a journal-less `retention:<job>` lease. Its events, each pool in its result and the daemon's log lines report the bytes freed (tracked files a remote holds and regenerable output, deleted without a copy) apart from the bytes moved into the archive and the bytes the archive adds (its bundle, manifest, summary and rows, and byte copies), with the net. A job whose baseline no network remote holds, and whose bundle would carry more than `retention.remote_less_history_bytes` (default 64 MiB) of history, is kept; a job registered in a repository inside another job's tree pins that job while it has rows. Retention's children run under the `utility` QoS clamp (C-5.1). Archives are kept until a person removes them.

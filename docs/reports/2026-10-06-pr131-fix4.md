The contract states:

> Automatic resolution is exactly as safe as `kill --confirm-dead`: the census is the same, no weaker and no stricter. Verified-empty quarantines release within one pace, including on the same boot; no reboot is required.

Every recorded live identity, recorded group member, and descendant holds.
Canonical cwd in or under the workdir and either census-visible marker also
hold. Every source must succeed before release. A census uses one process
table, one marker listing, and one cwd `lsof` call capped at ten seconds.

The observational residual is a writer never observed by lineage/groups or
parent walks, never observed with cwd in the workdir at a census, and running
a platform binary with hidden environment or removing both markers from
census-visible output (including by rewriting its title). No new session is
required. Sampling can miss a birth/orphaning or a cwd change entirely between
inspections. Retention's separate file-holder check covers an otherwise
invisible writer only while it actually holds a file, mapping, or directory;
append-and-close from an outside cwd can evade it.

## Real-process evidence and limits

The sandbox denies `/bin/ps` with `operation not permitted`, and kernel boot
inspection is unavailable. Consequently **none of the twenty full real census
hold/release cases ran**. They cover five process types, two detection paths
(observed lineage from outside cwd; cwd in the workdir), and two resolvers.
The hub must run them without this sandbox; no end-to-end real ownership or
release result is claimed here.

Earlier real `lsof` runs succeeded before the kernel-spelling refinement. All five direct cwd types were detected: detached `/bin/zsh`
append loop, `/bin/sleep`, `/usr/bin/tail -f`, node with `process.title`, and
a setsid shell. Each is detected under a symlink spelling and a nested cwd
with spaces/newlines; after stopping its exact pid/group and awaiting it, its
pid disappears from the scan. This proves cwd visibility, not release.

Seven foreground cwd listings contained **895–900 process records**, taking
**744–1,329 ms**, median **826 ms**. This is the accessible cwd population,
not a ps-verified count of all host processes. A later loaded run hit the
10-second cap repeatedly. The final affected slice and one isolated rerun both
timed out on all five cwd types before producing any listing. These are five
unresolved host failures on the final source, not positive final-source
visibility results. The readers were killed/reaped by `subprocess.run`,
raised inspection failure, and therefore hold. No retry was added to the
production reader. The hub must repeat both cwd and full-census real cases.

# Hold one task at admission, not whole lanes (D-TASKHOLD1)

2026-10-24. Branch `feat/task-hold-217`, from release/217 at `1c3bfc4f2`. Contract: C-11.10 (new),
with additions to C-6.11, C-16.2, C-17.1 and C-17.4, and a change-log entry under "Changes in
version 3".

## Why

A compute booking sometimes asks Subfleet to start no new builds; uk-data's batch releases do.
The hub's lane ramp could honour that only by keeping a provider's lanes closed while a build
waited on that provider, which parked every review, lookup and research job on those lanes too.
On 2026-10-24, before Fleet ops made build holds opt-in, one waiting build held all eight Claude
ramp lanes for hours. Max's rule since then: available model capacity should never sit idle
behind our own gates.

The daemon now holds the task at admission and leaves the lanes open for everything else. The
hub's ramp change, to set a task hold instead of holding lanes, is the hub's to make after this
ships and is not part of this build.

## The hub's decisions, as built

1. **Commands.** `subfleet tasks hold <task> --until <iso> --note <text>` and
   `subfleet tasks release <task>`, over a new socket op `tasks` (`protocol.TasksArgs`: `action`,
   `task`, `until`, `note`). `subfleet tasks` (or `tasks list`) lists the holds in force; it was
   not asked for, but costs one read and is how the CLI and the tests confirm a hold.
   - **Task names** are the keys of the policy's `chains`. That is the list the scheduler itself
     knows: submit refuses a task outside it (`daemon.submit`), and so does `scheduler.prepare`.
     `cli.TASK_CHOICES` is the CLI's copy and is shown in `--help`, but the daemon decides, so a
     policy that adds a task can hold it. A release also accepts a task the policy no longer
     routes, as long as its hold's row is still there, so a hold can always be ended.
   - **The note** is required: one line of printable characters (`str.isprintable`, so no
     newline, tab or control character), at most 160 characters once surrounding spaces are
     dropped. I added one condition the hub did not list: a note that C-23.14's scrubber would
     mask (`ids.holds_credential`) is refused too. The store never holds a secret (C-10.1), and
     `status` shows the note. One side effect: a single unbroken run of about 100 or more letters
     reads as a token and is refused. A prose note never is.
   - **`--until`** must be an ISO 8601 instant. One with no zone is read as UTC, as `lanes hold`
     reads it. It is kept to the whole second, and it must be later than now: an end already
     reached would hold nothing, so it is refused rather than written.
   - **A second hold** on a task replaces the first: the same row is overwritten, and the jobs the
     first hold kept waiting are re-clocked to the new end. **Release** ends the hold at once.
   - **Refusals** happen before any write, with closed templates (`refusals.HANDLER_MESSAGES`):
     `unknown-task`, `task-hold-note`, `task-hold-until` and `tasks-action`, all exit 2. The CLI
     also refuses a missing or malformed note or end before sending anything.
2. **Admission.** While a hold on task T is in force, no attempt starts for a job whose task is T
   and whose kind is not `turn`. That covers first attempts, retries, resumes, revives and
   handoffs.
   - The job waits with `wait_reason` `task-held` and `next_check_at` equal to the hold's end. Its
     admission hold (what `why` reads) is `{reason: task-held, task, until, note, next_check_at}`,
     and `why` prints: "the operator holds build jobs at admission until <end> (<note>); it starts
     once the hold ends or `subfleet tasks release build`, and holds no other job back (C-11.10)".
   - A release wakes the waiting jobs at once. In one transaction it deletes the hold, puts each
     held job back to `queued` and records `task.released`. It then bumps `_task_hold_epoch`, so
     a pass already in flight rereads the holds at its next job, and calls the daemon's
     `_notify`.
   - Turns are never held.
   - Running attempts are never read, signalled or re-placed: the hold and release handlers touch
     no attempt row, and only admission reads the hold.
   - Jobs of other tasks, and jobs with no task, are placed exactly as with no hold. A held job is
     inert: it is looked at first and no further.
   - One exception: a job waiting on a person (`approval`, `uncertain`) keeps that wait, so the
     hold never overwrites a quarantined probe's record. A person can cancel the job, or a
     verified-empty census can recover the probe automatically to `capacity`; admission then
     takes the active hold at its next look (fix round 5, R5-4a).
3. **Expiry needs no reaper.** `task_holds.in_force_now` compares the hold's end with the clock,
   and nothing deletes an expired row. A pass that finds a `task-held` job with no hold in force
   puts it back to `queued` and looks at it in the same pass.
4. **Storage: `leases`, no schema change.** A hold needs no table or column of its own. It was
   built at schema 7; release/217's migrations 8 and 9, merged in since, add only job and attempt
   columns. See the next section.
5. **Status.**
   - `daemon.status` (so `subfleet status --json`) and the offline `status` both carry
     `task_holds`: a list sorted by task, each entry with `task`, `until`, `note` and `since`,
     empty when there are none.
   - The `status` text shows a `task holds` section, each hold with its end, its note and the
     count of jobs it keeps waiting, only while a hold is in force.
   - The capacity text that `daemon.status` and `readings` render gains a `Task holds:` line.
   - A waiting job's `wait_reason` reads `task-held` in `runs`, `show`, the status rows and the
     menu's `status.json` rows.
   - A task hold raises no alert. `task-held` is in `NOT_ADMISSIONS_TO_PLACE`, so the
     idle-admission record (C-6.11) never counts a held job and never warns about one.
6. **Events.** `task.held` records `task`, `until`, `note` and `replaced` (the end and note of
   the hold in force that it replaced, or null). `task.released` records `task`, the ended
   hold's `until` and `note`, and `woken`. Both are rows of the append-only `events` table,
   written by the same transaction as the change, as `lane.held` is. A release when no hold
   exists changes no row, so it records nothing, the same as `lanes release`. Each job a hold
   catches or lets go adds its own `job.task_held` or `job.task_hold_ended` event.
7. **Contract.**
   - C-11.10 is the new admission clause.
   - C-6.11 gains the `task-held` reason.
   - C-16.2 and C-17.1 name the op and the verb.
   - C-17.4 is the status addition.
   - The "Changes in version 3" entry names D-TASKHOLD1.

## Storage choice: a `leases` row

A hold is one `leases` row:

- key `task-hold:<task>`;
- holder `operator: <note>`;
- `acquired_at`: when the hold was set;
- `expires_at`: its end.

The primary key makes "a second hold replaces the first" a single upsert. All reads are range
reads on that key (`lease_key >= 'task-hold:' AND lease_key < 'task-hold;'`), served by the
primary key's index.

I checked every reader, reaper, recovery path and census of `leases` in `subfleet/`. None treats
such a row as an attempt's or job's lease, or deletes it:

| Code | What it does with leases | Effect on a `task-hold:` row |
|---|---|---|
| `daemon.py` attempt end, finalization, quarantine release, `kill`, `_fail_queued`, retry, export (`DELETE … WHERE holder IN (attempt, job)`) | by holder | none: no attempt or job id starts with `operator: ` |
| `daemon.py` probe recovery and enrollment (`holder LIKE 'probe:%'`, `release_leases(probe holder)`) | by holder prefix | none |
| `daemon.py` resume and retire sweep (`lease_key LIKE 'retire:%' AND holder LIKE 'resume:%'`) | by key and holder prefix | none |
| `retention.py`, `retention_archive.py` (`holder LIKE 'retention:%'`, `holder='retention:<job>'`, census `job-lease` and `attempt-lease` joined on holder = job or attempt id, `worktree-lease`, `retire-lease`) | by holder or key prefix | none: the joins need holder = a job or attempt id |
| `gate/service.py` round lease | by its own key and holder | none |
| `store.release_leases(holder[, prefix])` | by holder | none |
| `daemon.PENDING_EXPORTS` (`job_id IN (SELECT holder FROM leases)`) | holder equals a job id | none |
| `folders.py`, `resource_leases.py`, `waits.py`, `lanes_transfer.py`, `operations.py`, `timers.py`, `capacity.PROBE_LEASES`, `conversations/*` | range or prefix reads on `worktree*:`, `out:`, `lane:`, `session:`, `conversation:`, `native:`, or holder `probe:` | none: no prefix overlaps `task-hold:` |
| `daemon._admit_pass` lease census `leases_now` (C-6.10's freed-capacity check) | **every** non-probe lease | Without a change, releasing a hold would read as freed capacity and bring backed-off capacity waits forward a pass early, so jobs of other tasks would be looked at on a different pass than with no hold, breaking T2. The census now excludes the `task-hold:` range. |
| `lanes` op, `pick`, the capacity view, slot counts | `lane:%` only | none |
| any reaper by `expires_at` | none exists | no reaper is needed or present |

No schema change, so no migration of its own. `test_a_hold_needs_no_schema_change_and_survives_a_restart`
reopens the store on a second connection, checks that it is at the store's current schema
(`SCHEMA_VERSION`, 9: release/217's migrations 8 and 9 add only job and attempt columns) with no
`task_hold` object, and reads the hold and the held job back.

## Admission audit: every placement path

`INSERT INTO attempts` occurs once in `subfleet/`: in `_admit_pass`'s reserving transaction
(`attempt.reserved`). Every placement path reaches it through the per-kind admission pass.

| Path | Where it is placed | Checks the hold? |
|---|---|---|
| First attempt of a detached job (`dispatch`, `handoff`, `resume`, `revive`) | detached pass | Yes, at the door: first in each job's look, before C-6.12's route wait, C-11.8's pin checks, C-6.13/17/18's guards, workspace preparation, routing and any probe. Then again inside the reserving transaction, at its own clock. |
| Retry after a failed attempt (C-4.5; the job is `queued` or `waiting` again) | detached pass | Yes, both checks. `test_a_held_build_retried_after_a_failed_attempt_waits` covers it. |
| Resume (`subfleet resume`): a new `resume` job carrying its source's `task` | detached pass | Yes, both checks. |
| Revive (`subfleet sessions revive`): a `revive` job carrying the task it was given | detached pass | Yes, both checks; a revive with no task is never held. |
| Gate round (`gate-review`): pinned model and lane, no task | detached pass | Checked, but a gate round has no task, so a task hold never holds one. |
| Conversation turn (`turn`) | turn pass | Checked, and `_task_hold_for` returns None for a turn: never held. |
| Admission probe (C-11.4) | `_prepare_route`, after the door | Not an attempt (a `probe:` lease). A held job never reaches it. A hold committed in the instant between a job's door check and its probe can let that one probe run; the reservation after it then refuses. |
| Timer probes, keepalive, heals (C-18.1) | timers | Not attempts, and no job's task: unaffected. |
| Another pass in flight | — | The pass rereads the holds at its next job once `_task_hold_epoch` changes (bumped after a hold or release commits), and the reserving transaction always rereads. A hold committed at any instant therefore stops every reservation that commits after it. |

## Invariants

- **T1:** no attempt is created for a non-turn job of task T while a hold on T is in force.
- **T2:** for jobs of any other task, admission decides exactly as it would with no hold.
  Stated so it can be tested exactly: with a hold on T in force, every other job is placed on the
  same pass, lane and model as in the same schedule with T's jobs and holds absent, under any
  caps. While held, T's jobs are inert: no waiter, no lease queue, no slot kept, no probe, no
  git. Once a hold ends, T's jobs compete for capacity again, which is intended.
- **T3:** after a release or the hold's end, every waiting T job is eligible on the next pass,
  and no job is cancelled, failed or lost because of a hold.
- **T4:** a hold never changes, signals or re-places a running attempt.
- **T5:** turns are placed exactly as with no hold. Tested more strongly: with no detached job of
  the held task present, every placement, turns included, is the same as with no hold.
- **Pure-reader properties** (`tests/unit/test_task_holds.py`):
  - a row is in force exactly while the clock is before its end;
  - only rows of the `task-hold:` key are read;
  - a note is kept exactly when it is one printable line within the limit, holding no credential;
  - an end is kept, to the second, exactly when it is later than now.

### The decisions, as built

1. **P2-1: pin grace pauses while held.** The grace counts only time the job was not held.
   - **Where it is kept.** C-11.8 keeps a grace in the job's `job.pin_unadmittable` event
     (`since`, `fail_at`), read back at start by `_load_pin_episodes`. A pause uses the same
     store. The hold that takes a job (`_take_for_hold`) writes `pin_paused_s` into the
     job's `job.task_held` event, marked `taken`: the grace left at the hold's start (its
     `since`, or the episode's start if later). The hold's end writes `pin_fail_at` into a
     `job.task_hold_ended` event: the end plus what was left. `_load_pin_episodes` reads both,
     so a restart keeps a paused grace paused, and an ended pause ends where it ended.
   - **"The end".** For a release, the release's instant. For an expiry, the hold's `until`,
     however late a pass or a restarted daemon notices it (`_task_hold_ended` takes the row's
     own clock, which is the `until`).
   - **Replacement.** A replacement made while the hold is in force keeps its `since`
     (`acquired_at`), and its jobs stay `task-held`, so the pause goes on. One that ends sooner
     ends the pause at its own `until`.
   - **Two holds that no pass saw apart.** If a hold ends unseen and another begins, the time
     between them counts against the grace, as time with no hold does. That happens in
     `_task_held` for a pass, or in `_release_task` for a release. The release case also covers
     a released hold that had itself ended unseen.
2. **P2-2: retry backoff kept apart from the hold.** The deadline is the `kept` field of the
   same `job.task_held` event, so `next_check_at` is still the hold's end, as before.
   - **What counts as a retry deadline.** The clock of a `capacity` wait whose latest attempt
     ended `transient`. That is C-4.5's backoff, the wait `_recover_capacity_waits` already
     leaves alone at a restart. The predicate is one constant, `RETRY_DEADLINE_SQL`.
   - **At release or expiry,** the job is next checked at `max(now, deadline)`: a waiting
     `capacity` row with the kept deadline while it is still ahead, else queued.
   - **Deviation from my first build of this, found by a test.** That build kept any clock the
     row had. `test_the_demand_returns_once_the_hold_is_released` failed with it. A job that
     waited on capacity with no attempt behind it had its recheck clock given back. Admission's
     in-memory capacity wait was gone, and nothing brought the job forward, so it was no reset
     demand until that clock. A recheck clock says only when to look again, and a look at the
     hold's end is as good, so only the retry deadline is kept. Every other job is queued at the
     end, as the build had it. `test_a_capacity_recheck_is_no_deadline_the_hold_keeps` pins
     this down.
3. **P2-3: a held job makes no reset demand.**
   - `_reset_demand` reads the capacity waits and the holds in force in one
     `store.snapshot()`, and leaves out every job a hold covers (`_demand_held`).
   - `ResetCredits` re-reads the job's hold in its pending transaction (`task_holds.job_held`,
     on the same connection) before the op-key check and any spend, and answers `demand-gone`
     with `task_held`.
4. **P3-1: offline counts.**
   - `task_holds.WAITING_SQL` counts each task's `task-held` jobs over the whole store.
     `listing(holds, waiting=…)` puts that count in each hold as `waiting`, online
     (`daemon.status`) and offline.
   - `format_status` prints that count. It falls back to counting listed rows only for a daemon
     that sends none: such a daemon lists every nonterminal job, so its count is whole too.
5. **P3-2: notes.** `task_holds.note_text` refuses a note with any control character
   (`CONTROLS`: C0, DEL, C1, U+2028, U+2029) anywhere in it, before it trims anything. It then
   trims ASCII spaces only. The CLI calls the same function before it sends anything, so
   `"\nbooking\n"` and `"\tbooking\t"` exit 2 with nothing sent or written.
6. **P3-3: release hints.** `task_holds.release_command(task)` is
   `shlex.join(["subfleet", "tasks", "release", "--", task])`. `why` uses it, and argparse
   reads `--` there as the end of options.
7. **Tests the review found thin.**
   - The property schedules gained four steps:
     - `pin`, a job pinned to a lane;
     - `enable`, a lane disabled or enabled, so C-11.8 episodes open and graces run;
     - `transient`, a live attempt ended through the real `_finalize`, so C-4.5's backoff runs;
     - `restart`, the daemon replaced by a successor on the same state root.
   - Each example runs with a grace of 60 s or 600 s.
   - The signal count is now read before the step's command. Only a finalizer's or a restart's
     own signals are the schedule's.
   - T2 and T5 now compare each pass's waiting states and deadlines (`waits`) as well as the
     placements.
   - Two new direct tests: an unknown task name is refused by name and holds nothing; and a
     release wakes a thread waiting on `changed` and pokes the wait hub after it commits (a
     spy on the daemon's real signals).
   - A property focused on T6, `test_t6_one_jobs_clock_under_random_holds`, added because the
     broad schedule property passed all 25 of its examples at `f6612e160`, before the fix: its
     new steps seldom line up into a hold over a running clock. This one starts with the clock
     running: one build with a 60 s or 600 s C-11.8 grace from t=0 (pinned to a disabled lane,
     or every lane disabled after a `transient` end), or C-4.5's backoff due at t=60. Then come
     random holds on `build` (replacements later and sooner), releases, restarts and clock
     steps, with a pass after each. It checks the job against a model of the held stretches:
     the job fails on the first pass that finds its grace, with the held time cut out, run out
     and no hold in force, and never before; its retry starts on the first pass at or after its

### Invariants T6 and T7

- **T6 (clock neutrality):** for any job and any sequence of holds on its task, the job's
  remaining pin grace and its retry deadline, at the moment it is next eligible, equal what they
  would be with the held intervals cut out of time. The property checks four things against a
  model of the held stretches:
  - while no hold keeps a job, its open episode's `fail_at` is
    `since + grace + (held time in [since, fail_at])`;
  - a job fails for its pin no earlier than that;
  - a retry a hold kept never starts before its deadline;
  - right after the hold ends, the job is due at that deadline, not later.

  The two clocks are cut differently because they are different kinds of clock. A grace is a
  length of time the lane gets to admit the job, so held time is taken out of it: 50 s of a 60 s
  grace are left after a hold from t=10, however long the hold. A retry's backoff is an instant
  (C-4.5: not before t=60), and with the held time cut out the instant is still t=60. So the
  hold keeps it and gives it back unchanged, and a hold that ends after it has satisfied it
  (decision 2: released at 15 or expired at 20, the retry starts at 60, as the unheld control
  does; held to 90, it starts at 90).
- **T7 (no demand while held):** no reset credit is spent for demand that consists only of held
  jobs. Two example scenarios, on the real timer with fake Codex endpoints, cover a hold
  committed before the timer's read and one committed between its last read and its spend. In
  the property schedules (added in this round's continuation), every step reads the timer's own
  `_reset_demand` right after its command, before the pass has looked at any job again, and
  again after the pass. Neither read may hold a job that a hold in force covers.
- **T3, restated:** no job is cancelled, failed or lost because of a hold. Since a pinned job may
  now fail for its own grace inside a schedule, the property allows one failure: C-11.8's
  (`job.pin_refused`, rc 3), and only once T6's model says the grace ran out.

### The decisions, as built

1. **F1: a hold pauses its jobs' clocks from its commit.**
   - **The gap.** `_hold_task` wrote only the lease row, and a job's pause was recorded only
     when an admission pass took it. A hold that ended before any pass charged all its time to
     the grace: r2's stuck job, held at t=10 with no pass, failed rc 3 at the hold's end.
   - **The fix.** `_take_task_jobs` runs inside the `task.held` transaction, as `_release_task`
     gives jobs back inside its own. It takes every job of the task that waits or is queued,
     except one waiting on a person (`approval`, `uncertain`) and one being cancelled. Each take
     writes its own `job.task_held` event before `task.held`.
   - **A job still held for an earlier hold that ended unseen** has that hold ended where it
     ended (`job.task_hold_ended`, `by: end`), then is taken. The time between the two holds
     counts against its grace, as before, but the job now leaves the commit on the new end.
     `test_the_grace_runs_between_two_holds_no_pass_saw_apart` used to assert that the job was
     left on the old end (t=30); it now asserts the new end (t=1000) and the ended event.
   - **The episode is read in the take's own transaction** (`_held_pin_episode`, through
     `_load_pin_episodes(read=...)`), because the take now runs on the socket's thread too. A
     pause that no end has given back carries over to a later take.
   - **The pass's memory of episodes.** The commit writes only the store. Once a hold or release
     has committed, the detached pass marks its jobs' open episodes stale, by the hold epoch, and
     reads each one again from the store at that job's door (`_pin_stale`). This replaces round
     1's check, which covered only a release.
   - **A look already under way when a hold commits** can still write the job's own wait over
     `task-held`. Such writes, outside the reserving transaction, include an opening C-11.8
     episode, a workspace or route deferral, and a probe's answer (C-11.4). The next pass takes
     the job again, and a grace the commit paused stays paused through that. The exception is a
     probe's answer that leaves the job waiting on a person (`uncertain`): it keeps that wait
     until a person cancels the job or a verified-empty census recovers the probe to `capacity` (corrected
     in fix round 5, R5-4a). The reserving transaction reads the hold again,
     as before, so no attempt starts. C-11.10 now says this.
2. **F2: only a retry deadline still ahead survives a hold.**
   - **The gap.** `kept` stored any capacity clock whose latest attempt ended `transient`, and
     the hold dropped `_capacity_waits`. A recheck long after the 60 s backoff came back as a
     deadline, and freed capacity no longer brought the job forward: r2's retry held at 92 and
     released at 93, its slot freed at 93, started at 121, while the unheld control started at 93.
   - **The fix.** `_kept_retry` keeps a clock only while the job waits on `capacity`, its latest
     attempt ended `transient` with no capacity steer (`RETRY_END_SQL`), and that attempt's end
     plus `TRANSIENT_RETRY_DELAY_S` is still ahead of the take. Otherwise nothing is kept, and the
     job is queued at the hold's end. `RETRY_END_SQL` replaces round 1's `RETRY_DEADLINE_SQL`,
     which nothing else read. A steered transient (C-9.5) has no backoff, so it keeps nothing.
   - **A refinement of the decision's wording.** The deadline decides *whether* a clock is kept;
     the value kept is the job's own clock, not the attempt's end plus 60 s. While that deadline
     is ahead, no look has given the job a recheck, because a retry's wait has no capacity wait
     for freed capacity to bring forward. So its clock is the one the finalizer set, or one a
     C-11.8 episode set, whose grace the hold pauses and gives back first. The finalizer sets
     its clock from its own instant, to the whole second, not from the provider's exit time.
     Keeping the job's own clock gives back exactly what the unheld job would wait for. The fake

1. **R3-1: no admission write overrides a committed hold.**

       and the job failed at 70, and an expiry at 1000 seen at 1030 failed it at once.
   - **The fix.** `Daemon._task_held_at_write(tx, job, holds)` reads the task's hold inside the
     transaction that is about to write. If a hold covers the job, that transaction takes it for
     the hold instead, as the commit's take would, and writes nothing of its own. Every
     admission transaction that writes a job's wait, opens its episode or fails it on a clock

       hold committed: it found the first hold ended and gave the job its grace back over the

## Fix round 4 (review r4)

### Decisions as built

1. **R4-1, the door's take and move.** Both transactions call `_task_held_at_write` inside
   their writer. That helper reads the current hold, takes or moves only that hold, and writes
   nothing if a release or expiry ended it. The copied door hold is never the writer's authority.
   The two barrier-controlled inputs now keep the release's grace to 70 and the replacement's
   grace to 1550 after release at 1500, rather than extending it to 90 or failing at 1500.
2. **R4-2, legacy retry deadlines.** New attempts still keep exactly `retry_due_at`. For an
   attempt finalized before that field existed, `_kept_retry` reads its own `attempt.accepted`
   event, by `attempt_id`, and adds `TRANSIENT_RETRY_DELAY_S`. Neither `finished_at` nor the job's
   mutable `next_check_at` supplies that deadline. There is no migration or new column.
   - **Code check before changing it:** `_finalize` calculates `next_check` with `after` inside
     `Store.transaction("attempt.accepted")`; after the transaction body returns, the store
     inserts the event with `utc_now`, then commits. Both `after` and `utc_now` use
     `datetime.now(timezone.utc)`, with second-resolution serialization. The event is not stamped
     before the deadline calculation and does not use a different clock.
   - **Precision caveat:** the code does not bound the elapsed time between those two wall-clock
     reads. A one-second rollover gives the permitted 161 rather than 160; an arbitrarily stalled
     finalizer could give a later fallback while UTC runs forward. The hub's event-plus-delay rule is built
     unchanged; the claim of a universal one-second upper bound is not made here. Wall-clock
     rollback is likewise not prevented by these existing clocks.
   - The legacy fixture executes the real finalizer and audit, then removes only `retry_due_at`
     to represent the pre-round-3 evidence shape before reopening the daemon. The example pins
     exit 0, finalization 100, hold 110 and release 111; the property covers delays 0, 1, 40 and
     100. While the UTC wall clock runs forward, the fallback is never earlier than the finalizer's
     deadline. It is later by the finalizer's time from reading the deadline to committing, rounded
     up to the second, so a held legacy retry starts at most 60 s late. A clock stepped back between
     the two reads makes it earlier by the step; the labelled round-5 pin starts at 155 against 160.
3. **R4-3, clocks after the writer.** Hold and release sample `utcnow` only after entering
   their transaction. Hold-until validation, replacement, expiry, `since`, release restoration
   and `was_held` all use that effective clock. A real finalizer keeps the writer until 50 in
   the regression tests: a hold requested at 10 and released at 100 restores 110, not 150;
   a release requested at 20 restores 100, not 70. Both threaded runs finish without deadlock.
4. **R4-4, quarantine's two endings.** Corrected in round 5: C-11.10 and the round-3 report state

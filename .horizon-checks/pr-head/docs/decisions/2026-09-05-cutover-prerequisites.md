## Read-only canary execution, enforced (peer rounds 1 and 2)

`--sandbox read-only` restrains model-generated shell commands and nothing else, as the peer noted; MCP servers, plugins, and hooks configured in the Codex home would still act. Every canary job is therefore submitted through the isolated-review path, `sf2 run -m astra -s read-only -I -D <clone> -C <clone> -p <prompt> -n canary-NNN`, which the repository already uses for gate reviews: the Codex adapter inspects the lane's config layers and MCP inventory and launches with `--ephemeral --ignore-user-config --ignore-rules`, `features.hooks=false`, `features.plugins=false`, the rest of `CODEX_CONFIG`, and an explicit disable for every named MCP server (C-23.2, C-23.4, `adapters/isolation.py`); an inventory that cannot be inspected refuses the launch. The workdir and review root are a dedicated clone, `~/subfleet-v2-canary/work`, of this repository, never one of Max's live checkouts. Prompts are the text of 100 `prompt.md` files sampled evenly from the v1 ledger's 499, each prefixed with a one-line canary preamble; the canary measures v2's machinery, not the prompts' original tasks. `tools/canary_check.py` asserts, from the store and the recorded cohort in `~/subfleet-v2-canary/jobs.json`: the cohort is exactly the submitted job ids; every attempt's `launch.json` argv carries `--sandbox read-only` and the three isolation flags and both feature disables; no attempt is `quarantined` or `lost`; the clone is clean with HEAD at the recorded baseline. Acceptance is covered in the next section.

## Canary acceptance (peer round 2)

A batch that only fails cleanly proves nothing about success paths. The gate requires at least 90 of the 100 jobs to have succeeded, where success means an accepted attempt with a deliverable artifact of non-zero size and a notice row for the job (so classification, attestation recording, deliverable capture, and notice creation each ran), and the remaining jobs to have ended with a classified failure (`limited`, `auth-dead`, `cli-too-old`, `content-filter`, `transient`), never `unknown`. `tools/canary_check.py` prints `PENDING` until the cohort is terminal, `PASS` only when the floor is met and there is no violation, `FAIL` otherwise; the floor is a parameter (`--min-success`), and the record in `docs/release-gates.md` states the value used. Tested in `tests/unit/test_soak_tools.py`.

## Soak evidence (peer rounds 1 and 2)

The soak is seven days from the transfer, recorded as `since` in `~/.subfleet/soak.json` at the moment of the transfer. `tools/soak_report.py` counts only attempts reserved at or after `since` and never rows the importer wrote from v1's ledger (their `evidence_json` carries `imported: true`); v1's historical losses appear in a separate "history" section and cannot stop the clock. The daily record, `docs/soak/<date>.md`, holds: attempts by terminal state (today and since the start); every `quarantined` or `lost` attempt in scope with its census evidence; jobs with more than one succeeded attempt (must be zero); actions left `unknown` or stuck over an hour (must be zero); the events that fired that day by kind, so the record shows the timers ran; canary progress. The stop rule: any item in the must-be-zero lists pauses the soak clock until the cause is written down in the record; seven clean days pass the gate. The report runs daily at 09:00 from a launchd agent installed for the soak and removed after, and by hand on the day of the transfer and on the morning after Monday's reset. Tested in `tests/unit/test_soak_tools.py` (an imported loss is history; a fresh loss stops; an attempt before `since` is out of scope).

## Questions the plan should answer before milestone 1

1. **What exact evidence authorizes releasing a workspace after guardian or daemon failure?** A terminal database label is insufficient if another process can still write.

2. **What is the persisted transition protocol for submit, spawn, bind, cancel, receipt, export, and notice?** Every external action needs a defined interrupted-state recovery path.

3. **Who generates and retains the request ID, and what happens when its payload differs?** Restart-safe deduplication depends on caller behavior absent from the five-line contract.

4. **What are the actual lane and native-session identities?** Homes, account bindings, credential epochs, and transcript roots can change independently.

5. **What is the complete capacity precedence table?** It must distinguish unsupported probes, network failure, temporary throttling, authentication failure, model rejection, shared limits, stale readings, and reset confirmation.

6. **Which protections must pass before the first writable Codex job?** Deferring guard parity to milestone 8 conflicts with the promise to preserve incident safeguards from the start.

7. **What makes automatic revival exclusive against desktop launches?** A lock used only by subfleet cannot establish ownership over an external launcher.

8. **How are long-running and restarted sessions awakened after the private socket is removed?** Completion waiters alone do not cover manual ping, tickle, or muster.

9. **What exact state and execution ownership transfer at each cutover and rollback step?** This determines whether old agents, active jobs, gates, notices, and credit consumption remain correct.

10. **What are the concrete probe, job, and retention budgets?** These determine whether the rebuild avoids another capacity sweep and whether maintenance traffic erases useful history.
